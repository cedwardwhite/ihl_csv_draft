Title:
Ichikawa Danjūrō IX as Yamanoue Gennaizaemon in the play Zōhiki (九世市川団十郎 山上源内左衛門・象引)
Series:
The Kabuki Eighteen (歌舞伎十八番 Kabuki jūhachiban)
Creator:
Torii Kiyosada 鳥居清貞 (1844-1901)
Torii Kiyotada VII 七代 鳥居清忠 (1875-1941)
Narrative Date:
Published May 25, 1896 (Meiji 29) 明治廿九年五月廿五日發行 as printed left margin
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1896
Description:
Ichikawa Danjūrō IX, in the bottom right panel, in the role of Yamanoue Gennaizaemon in the kabuki play Zōhiki (Pulling the Elephant). Pictured is a tug-of-war between Soga no Iruka (played by an unidentified actor) and Gennaizaemon, who are using an elephant instead of a rope. The elephant pulling scene comes at "the climatic point . . . when the chief male characters pose dramatically as they attempt to pull an elephant apart (the prop for the elephant is about five feet from tail to trunk and looks like an overstuffed toy.)"[1]
The Ichikawa family crest (mon) of nested squares is shown to the left of Danjūrō.
"The Kabuki Eighteen," a collection of plays established by Ichikawa Danjūrō VII, stresses the special aragoto acting art of the Danjūrō line. In the Meiji period other acting families began to gather their most successful plays into similar collections.[2]
"This particular series of prints of Danjūrō VII's famous selection is the most well-known work by Torii Kiyotada (who is also confusingly known as Tadakiyo or Hasegawa Kanpei XIV), and was based on sketches by his father, Torii Kiyosada. Kiyotada was himself an amateur kabuki actor, and probably had a deep personal interest in the series."[3]
The series was first published in 1895-1896 by the publisher Hasegawa Sumi and a second edition was released in 1927 by the publisher Shūbisha. Shūbisha issued the series in nine sets of two prints each, enclosed in an envelope. These reissued prints lack the original publisher's name and related information that appeared in the left column on the original Hasegawa release.

[1] The play was performed as part of the drama Keisei Osho-kun written in 1701 by Ichikawa Danjūrō I under his pen name Mimasuya Hyōgo.
[2] Kabuki Eighteen Traditional Dramas, Kawatake Toshio, Chronicle Books, 1885, p. 55

Miscellaneous:
Embossing throughout background. The two square seals beneath the publisher's name, 長谷川寿美, are associated with a number of publishers during the 1890s, including Hasegawa Sumi 長谷川寿美 and Komori Sōjirō 小森宗次郎. Taken together, the seals read Kumiai seigen 組合正言. Kumiai can be translated as "guild" and seigen can be translated as "censor" (using the Chinese meaning for the word.) So, we may have some type of publisher's guild approval or censor seal.
