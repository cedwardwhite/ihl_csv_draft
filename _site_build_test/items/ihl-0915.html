Title:
Singing Monkey on a Bare Tree (枯木吟猿)
Series:
Seihō's Masterpieces, set 1 (栖鳳逸品集 第1期 Seihō ippin shū dai-1-ki)
Creator:
Takeuchi Seihō 竹内栖鳳 (1864-1942)
Narrative Date:
November 10, 1938 - as printed in Japanese on colophon for envelope 9 of 10, set 1, containing the print
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1938
Description:
One of three prints in the ninth of ten envelopes containing the prints comprising set one.
Seihō's Masterpieces consists of sixty-six limited edition prints issued by Unsōdō Publishing over five years, beginning in April 1937 and ending in June 1942, produced from drawings made by the Kyoto artist Takeuchi Seihō (1864-1942). Using multiple printing techniques such as color woodblock and collotype, Unsōdō spared no effort in faithfully rendering into large-format prints Seihō’s watercolor and ink drawings. Jack Hillier in "The Art of the Japanese Book", 1987, has called these works "one of the most magnificent printing achievements of the twentieth century."
Seiho's Masterpieces includes scenes from the artist's travels to South China in 1921-1922, renderings of animals (domestic and wild), still-lifes of fish and flora, depictions of cultural events such as festival floats and kabuki, and landscapes of the Japanese countryside, including two prints depicting Mt. Fuji.
While there are no historical records explaining how this project came about, other than the 1937 Preface to Set One and the 1940 Publisher's Foreword to Set Two, it is reasonable to assume, given the artist’s declining health when the series was started in 1937 and his receipt of the Order of Culture in early 1937, that it was truly intended by his long-time publisher Unsōdō, in the words Roger S. Keyes, as an “elegy and a testament” to one of the greatest painters and teachers of his time., issued as one of four prints in the first of nine envelopes containing the prints comprising set two.
