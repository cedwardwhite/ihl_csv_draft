Title:
Chūzenji, Utagahama (中禅寺 歌ヶ浜)
Creator:
Kawase Hasui 川瀬巴水 (1883-1957)
Narrative Date:
1931 original date of publication as shown bottom of left margin. This collection's print a later printing (after WWII but likely before the artist's death in 1957) from the original blocks. This print does not carry a seal of the publisher Watanabe.
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1931
Description:
"This is the highest altitude lake in Japan, offering an escape from the heat and humidity of summer. Two pilgrims approach the vermillion gate that marks the entrance to Chūzenji, one of the thirty-three temples dedicated to the Kannon of Mercy on the eastern Japan pilgrimage route. The day is bright and clear, yet the scene is typically timeless; nothing infringes on the natural scenery." - Source: Didactics by Lynn Katsumoto for the exhibition "Ukiyo-e to Shin Hanga: Changing Tastes in Japanese Woodblock Prints" (Portland Japanese Garden, November 20, 2021 - January 30, 2022)
A view of Utagahama on the east side of Lake Chūzenji, near Nikkō. This design was one of 111 by Hasui that were shown in the second shin hanga exhibition at the Toledo Museum of Art in 1936. The catalog entry for the 1936 show notes "Blocks, 20; superimposed printings, about 25."
From 1918 until the end of his life, Hasui produced over 600 designs for prints, traveling the country in search of places that retained the character and appearance of the Japan he knew as a child. He sketched outdoors, mostly with pencil, later adding colors and effects when back in his lodgings.
