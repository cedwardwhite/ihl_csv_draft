Title:
On Stage in September at the Asakusaza: A scene from The Miraculous Cripple’s Revenge at Hakone (浅草座九月きょうげん｜箱根霊験躄仇討)
Creator:
Toyohara Kunichika 豊原国周 (1835–1900)
Narrative Date:
1893 (Meiji 16) - 明治十六年 月 日 as printed in publisher double-fash shaped cartouche.
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1893
Description:
Starring Ichikawa Kuzō III (R), Nakamura Shikan IV & Onoe Taganojō II (C) and Sawamura Tosshi VII (L)
"Revenge stories were a mainstay of the kabuki theater, fulfilling the human desire to see justice done. Often, the basic plot was a familiar one, though each season the theaters offered new elements, new twists, even new characters to tantalize fans. In this case, the play was based on vengeance for a 16th-century murder. The scene shows the wife of the hero, who had been crippled by disease, standing under the icy waters of a sacred waterfall, praying for his cure. Only later is it revealed that the faithful wife had herself been murdered, leaving her ghost to continue her ritual supplications.
"The triptych format served as an excellent device for depicting a scene on the kabuki stage, allowing stylized actor portraits (their names and roles identified in the narrow vertical cartouches) and scenery to contextualize the action. Each sheet could be sold separately. Kabuki fans collected the prints as mementos, while theater owners often co-sponsored this type of publicity to promote their productions." - Source: Didactics by Lynn Katsumoto for the exhibition "Ukiyo-e to Shin Hanga: Changing Tastes in Japanese Woodblock Prints" (Portland Japanese Garden, November 20, 2021 - January 30, 2022)
