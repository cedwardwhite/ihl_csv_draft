Creator:
Ishii Hakutei 石井柏亭 (1882-1958)
Narrative Date:
December 16, 1918
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1918
Description:
This print, one of 15 contributed by Hakutei to the 50 print series published by Nakajima Jūtarō, pictures laborers unloading what appear to be some type of tile from skiffs on the Taedong River in South Pyongan Province of North Korea.
To the left of the title, the following verse, transcribed in simplified Chinese, is inscribed:
長城一面溶々水 大野東頭點々山
长城一面溶溶水,大野东头点点山
The foot of the moss-grown old rampart/Is washed by blue streams.
Dawn is breaking over the vast fields/And the rows of mountains.
The verse is attributed to the 11th century Koryo dynasty poet Kim Hwang Won (1045-1117) who, it is said, broke his brush and cried, as words failed him to describe the beauty of the view along the Taedong River.
Japan annexed Korea in August 1910 and occupied it until 1945. Hakutei visited Korea in 1910, as did other Japanese artists, to tour the area and exhibit his work in Seoul. As was the fashion, it is likely he participated in what came to be called "sketch tours" during his travels, and that the five prints he created in this Set 8 of the series, subtitled Korea, were based upon sketches he made during his visit.
"The series Nihon fūkei hanga (Japan Scenery Prints) consisted of ten sets of prints, with each set composed of five small landscapes and a cover print giving the name of the series, the name and number of the set, and the name of the artist. In addition, each set was accompanied by a table of contents and colophon sheet. Specific publication dates ranging from January 1917 through April 1920 indicate that each set was issued separately. As Ishii Hakutei (1882-1958) designed the majority of prints in the series, it seems likely that Hakutei was the spearhead for the project. The artists asked Igami Bonkutsu to carve the blocks and Nakajima Jūtarō to publish the series." - Source: Modern Japanese Woodblock Prints - The Early Years, Helen Merritt, University of Hawaii Press, 1998, p. 274-276.
In addition to Ishii Hakutei (1182-1958), the other artists who contributed to the series were Sakamoto Hanjirō (1882-1969), see this collection's print Sea of Fire, IHL.0862 and Chikugo River, IHL.2068, Hirafuku Hyakusui (1877-1933), Morita Tsunetomo (1881-1933), Kosugi Misei (1881-1964) and Ishii Tsuruzō (1887-1973), elder brother of Hakutei.
