Title:
Kogō 小督 (Lady Kogō)
Series:
Encyclopedia of Noh Plays (能画大鑑 Nōga taikan)
Creator:
Tsukioka Kōgyo 月岡耕漁 (1869-1927)
Narrative Date:
The series was originally published between 1925 and 1930. In 1936 and again in 1937 it was republished (a "revised edition" 改版) under the title Nōga taikan: nōga nihyakuban ōzoroe (能畫大鑑 : 能畫貮百番大揃, Nōga taikan: 200 prints of Noh play), in which the prints were bound into a single volume. A specific publishing date for this print cannot be determined.
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1925
Description:
Play synopsis A Guide to No, P.G. O'Neill, Hinoki Shoten, 1929, p. 82) - A Court official announces that he has been ordered to send Nakakuni to find the Lady Kogō, the favorite of the Emperor Takakura, who has disappeared from Kyōto for fear that, since the Empress is the daughter of Taira no Kiyomori, she would incur his displeasure if she remained in the capital. As there has been word that Kogō is living near Sagano, Nakakuni goes there and is eventually led to the lady by the sound of a Japanese harp being played. He gives Kogō a letter from the Emperor and, after receiving her reply, prepares to return. But pitying Kogō in her unhappiness, he drinks some wine with her and dances in an attempt to console her. He then takes leave of the lady and sets off back to the capital.

Nōga Taikan (Encyclopedia of Noh Plays), made up of 200 prints, was published in Tokyo between 1925 and 1930 in five volumes. The original designs for Volumes 2 through 5 were drawn by Kōgyo, but the drawings for twenty-four of the prints in Volume 1 were created after Kōgyo’s death by Matsuno Sōfū 松野奏風 (1899-1963), Kōgyo’s very talented protégé.
"Each print is a scene from the noh play represented, similar to the same scene as it appeared on stage. Of Kōgyo’s three sets of noh prints, the Nōga Taikan prints seem to show the plays more accurately than either Nōgaku zue or Nōgaku hyakuban. In fact, if he sketched plays in the noh theater using brush and black ink, as his daughter Gyokusei said he did, he must have made notes to himself somewhere about what colors and designs to use on the costumes and stage props. The prints are very simple: the main actors at an important moment in the play, the artist’s signature, and the artist’s seal. The prints reveal at most few parts of the stage and its backdrop, and when so, only faintly. The focus is on the actors qua actors. The prints do not reveal the name of the plays represented, but each print in all five volumes is preceded by a piece of translucent paper on which the title of the play, the actors by type (shite, waki, tsure, kyōgen, kokata) and name of character in his role appear in Japanese." - Source: "Kōgyo, The Art of Noh," University of Pittsburgh https://exhibit.library.pitt.edu/kogyo/noga_taikan.html [accessed 8-17-23]

Printer:
Printers for the 1936 edition were Yoshida Takesaburō 吉田竹三郎 and Uchida Eikichi 岡田(打田)英吉. Eikichi is known for his pioneering work in woodblock machine printing using a modified letterpress machine. The listed printer for the 1925 edition, volumes 2-5, is 田中茜助 (name unread).
