Description:
Kawakami pictures the torii at the still controversial Yasukuni Shrine honoring Imperial Japan's war-dead. Issued in 1946, a little over a year after Japan's surrender, as part of the fifteen print series Tokyo Kaiko Zue (Scenes of Lost Tokyo), it was only the lax oversight of the print arts in general by the US Occupation censor's eye that allowed the series not only to be published but to be sold in the US military Post Exchanges.

SCENES OF LAST TOKYO (TŌKYŌ KAIKŌ ZUE)
Tōkyō kaikō zue 東京回顧圖會, a portfolio of fifteen prints created by nine artists, is variously translated as Scenes of Last Tokyo ("last" likely being a typographical error appearing on the folder accompanying the complete collection), Recollections of Tokyo, Retrospective Scenes of Tokyo, and Scenes of Lost Tokyo. Though the target audience for the portfolio was the Occupation forces, the portfolio, according to Lawrence Smith, contained "a coded message to Japanese readers" in the subjects depicted in some of the prints and in its introductory message. This coded message, composed shortly after the surrender of the Japanese in August 1945, by the leading sōsaku hanga (creative print) artist Onchi Kōshirō (1891-1955), was a hope for the retention of some of the imperial institutions in the remaking of Japan. - Source: Japanese Prints During the Allied Occupation 1945 – 1952, Lawrence Smith, The British Museum Press, 2002, p. 23–24.
