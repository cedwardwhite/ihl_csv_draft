* 御届 - notification delivered of intent to publish (otodoke)
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1885
Description:
Print number 31 (卅一), and one of 20 designs contributed by Kiyochika to the series Instructive Models of Lofty Ambition, portraying ten year old Tokugawa Takechiyo (1543-1916), later to become Tokugawa Ieyasu, founder of the Tokugawa Shogunate, "on the back of his retainer, seemingly directing a mock battle of children throwing stones along the banks of the Anegawa. Rocks can be seen in mid-flight. The original anecdote behind this print has the ten year old Ieyasu not directing the battle, but rather commenting on the battle, to the effect that the more organized and motivated, although smaller, group would win.
"On arriving, he said to those in attendance, 'Don’t watch the majority side. It’s best to be on the other side.'
"This was then seen as an indication of the young Ieyasu’s precocious wisdom and prowess in battle, and evidence of 'a divine gift with which Ieyasu was endowed at birth.'
"The story comes from the c. 1640 scroll titled the "Tōshō Daigongen engi". The scroll describes the origins of this “popular game” of the time and then the specific incident." - source: The Eyes of Power: Art and Early Tokugawa Authority, Karen M. Gerhart, University Of Hawai'i Press, 1999. P. 122.

INSTRUCTIVE MODELS OF LOFTY AMBITION
This didactic series was published between October 1885 and November 1890 and featured a long list of heroes and heroines, from antiquity to contemporary times, who were regarded as standards of moral leadership and self-realization. Originally envisioned as a 50 print series, it would grow to 58 prints. Seven artists contributed to the series and upon its completion the publisher, Matsuki Heikichi, was singled out for special recognition by the government for having sponsored such "noble subject matter."
