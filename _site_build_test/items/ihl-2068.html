Description:
This print by Sakamoto Hanjirō from the series Japan Scenery Prints, Set 6: Tsukushi District, presents a semi-abstract view of the Chikugogawa in a heavy rain. The Chikugo River flows through Kumamoto, Ōita, Fukuoka and Saga prefectures in Japan. With a total length of 143 kilometres (89 miles), it is the longest river in Kyūshū. It flows from Mount Aso and empties into the Ariake Sea.
"Tsukushi is the ancient name of the area north-west of Kyushu [in the area of Chikuzen and Chikugo provinces] where Sakamoto was born. Sakamoto was rarely involved with sheet-prints, but like his colleagues on this series he had worked as a cartoonist and illustrator and had been a collaborator on the magazine Hōsun hence he was well used to graphic techniques. Indeed, his set [Tsukushi district] is the most strikingly designed in the series, combining impressionist, abstract and Expressionistic elements into a characteristically forceful blend." - Source: Modern Japanese Prints 1912-1989, Lawrence Smith, British Museum Press, 1994, p. 42.

JAPAN SCENERY PRINTS
"The series Nihon fūkei hanga (Japan Scenery Prints) consists of ten sets of prints, with each set composed of five small landscapes and a cover print giving the name of the series, the name and number of the set, and the name of the artist. In addition, each set was accompanied by a table of contents and colophon sheet. Specific publication dates ranging from January 1917 through April 1920 indicate that each set was issued separately. As Ishii Hakutei (1882-1958) designed the majority of prints in the series, it seems likely that Hakutei was the spearhead for the project. The artists asked Igami Bonkutsu to carve the blocks and Nakajima Jūtarō to publish the series." - Source: Modern Japanese Woodblock Prints - The Early Years, Helen Merritt, University of Hawaii Press, 1998, p. 274-276.
In addition to Sakamoto Hanjirō (1882-1969), the other artists who contributed to the series were Ishii Hakutei (1182-1958), see IHL.1182 and IHL.1183, Hirafuku Hyakusui (1877-1933), Morita Tsunetomo (1881-1933), Kosugi Misei (1881-1964) and Ishii Tsuruzō (1887-1973), elder brother of Hakutei.
