Description:
Originally issued in 1938, this collection's print is 1960 re-printing, possibly from re-carved blocks, mounted on a decorative cardboard shikishiban sheet. The verso of the print carries a seal reading "quality control" 品質保証 along with the characters 華 and 靑.
ABOUT THE SERIES "THE STORY OF THE CORMORANT"
"When he made this series of 31 Prints, Munakata was inspired by the centuries-old No play of the same title. The image depicts the wife of a hunter, who unbeknown to her, has been condemned to become a devil to atone for taking the life of fellow creatures. The wife is shown opening her door to a monk, the bearer of the message and relics from her husband, who can no longer return to her. Munakata has eloquently captured this fleeting moment in the wife's inquiring expression. Most of the woodblocks of this 1938 series were lost. This is one of the few to survive. - Source: website of the Art Gallery New South Wales https://www.artgallery.nsw.gov.au/collection/works/DO28.1968/#about
THE ARTIST'S COMMENTS
“When I began to work on the subject, I tried to reproduce the basic verse and the three basic rhythms of the noh music by means of hanga. I was beginning to see the importance of expression in black and white and wanted to explore the possibility of the medium. The nature of noh – the very Japaneseness of its expression; its art of movement, calmness, flow and spaciousness; the tension created by the drums and flute; and the mysterious harmony of them all – is very akin to that of a woodblock picture…I set the scene in Aomori and chose the local people as cast. I avoided the noh setting, and aimed to express the story as a sad, melancholic sentiment of the snowy country.” Source: Munakata Shikō Zenshū (The Complete Works of Shiko Munakata), Volume 1, Kodansha, 1978, p. 116.
