Description:
A twenty print (all color lithographs) kamishibai (picture card show) authorized and sponsored during the Pacific War by the Nihon Kyōiku Kamishibai Kyōkai (Japanese Association for Educational Kamishibai), "a quasi-governmental organization charged with producing plays in support of Japanese government policies, to be performed domestically and throughout the Japanese Empire and conquered territories." - Source: https://networks.h-net.org/node/20904/discussions/10774246/kamishibai-database [accessed 8-17-23]
During a picture card show, the presenter would show each image while reciting, sometimes ad-libbing, the accompanying text on each card's verso. For wartime propaganda picture card shows the presenter was required to carefully follow the script and not ad-lib.
While a picture card show associated with children's street entertainment, may seem innocent enough, picture card shows used for war propaganda purposes were targeted not only at children but also at "adults, particularly those of lower classes: factory workers, farmers and other agricultural workers, inhabitants of poor urban neighborhoods, and small business owners, among others." - Source: Propaganda Performed, Kamishibai in Japan's Fifteen Year War, Sharalyn Orbaugh, Brill, 2015, p. 2.
Post-war, wartime kamishibai were the subject of an investigation by the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (IMTFE), better known as the Tokyo War Crimes trial. During this investigation into Japanese wartime propaganda, Akio Saki, President of the Nihon Kamishibai Association, was called as a prosecution witness. The investigation briefly traced the development of educational kamishibai 教育紙芝居 which were originally used, according to Akio, "to make children be honest, kind and to study their lessons," but would later be used as war propaganda (a wartime kamishibai titled "Japan Is Now Fighting" was referenced) "made on direct orders from various branches of the government and many others also by orders from Imperial Rule Assistance Association." Source: website of the University of Virginia School of Law The International Military Tribunal For The Far East Digital Collection, Transcript of proceeding of June 21, 1946 p. 1129- 1187 http://imtfe.law.virginia.edu/collections/morgan/4/1/trial-transcript-june-21-1946 [accessed 8-17-23]
