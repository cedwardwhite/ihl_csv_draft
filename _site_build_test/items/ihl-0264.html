Ichikawa Sanshō V as a Sweetmeat Peddler in the play Uirō (五世市川三升 外郎売)

1926
Description:
One of thirty-six prints making up the Watanabe-published series Collection of Creative Print Portraits by Shunsen (1925-1929). This print is the series installment no. 15. The character 壽 kotobuki (long life) is written on the actor's fan and refers to the medicine he is peddling.
"Ichikawa Sanshō X* appears full-figure, early in his career, in a pose established long before for the role of the fast-talking peddler. Bearing on his back a pack full of moxa – Japanese mogusa, a medicine to be burned, like incense, on the skin of the patient – Sanshō faces the audience and holds out a sample as he delivers a long sales pitch filled with puns, jokes, and other remarks."- source: Dramatic Impressions: Japanese Theatre Prints from the Gilbert Luber Collection, Chance, Frank L. & Davis, Julie Nelson, University of Pennsylvania Press, 200, p. 41.
*While the actor pictured is identified as Ichikawa Sanshō X, the Kabuki21 website (see http://www.kabuki21.com/sansho5.php [accessed 8-17-23] the Ichikawa Sanshō lineage ended with Ichikawa Sanshō V, who held the name from November 1917 until his death in February 1956.
