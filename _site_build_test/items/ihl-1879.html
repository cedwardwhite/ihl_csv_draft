Title:
Fūzoku gahō, Issue 359 (風俗画報・参百五十九號)

Editor:

Narrative Date:
March 10, 1907 - 明治四十年三月十日 発行 as printed in table of contents.
Earliest Date of Creation or Publication:
1907
Description:
The cover illustration, titled Gentle Spring Rain 春雨, for this March 1907 issue of the magazine Fūzoku gahō (Illustrated Customs), was designed by Kūrosaki Shusai 黒崎修齋 (1877-?), a painter and illustrator who was a frequent contributor to the magazine. The two other artists contributing color illustrations to this issue were Yamamoto Shōun 山本松谷 (1870-1955) whose color lithograph double-page frontispiece, titled "Ashio Copper Mine Miners' Violence" 足尾銅山坑夫の暴擧, depicts the miners' attack on mine officials while buildings burn and Sakamaki Kōgyo 坂巻耕漁 (1869-1927), better known as Tsukioka Kōgyo 月岡耕漁, whose print depicts a scene in the Noh play Utaura (Poem Divination).

Fūzoku gahō was a Meiji-early Taishō period illustrated magazine, published from 1889-1916 covering a variety of topics related to folk customs. The magazine described itself as relating "customs of the nation-state, both old and new." - Source: Splendid Monarchy: Power and Pageantry in Modern Japan, Takashi Fujitani, University of California Press, 1996, p. 184.
And, from the Tuesday, July 17, 1900 issue of the Paris, Kentucky "The Bourbon News," the article "A Japanese Magazine," relates the following:
"The Fuzoku Gaho is profusely illustrated in various ways - double-paged pictures confined to single subjects, or made up of many sketches, as we find them in weekly numbers of the Graphic etc. This system is most frequently resorted to where several classes of the community are engaged in the same occupation or when the old and new version of existing things are set off against each other by way of contrast. Single-paged pictures in black and white or colors, and also small sketches interspersed in the text are comprised within the covers of this monthly. The Fuzoku Gaho embraces a variety of subjects of historic worth and present interest - ancient manners and customs that are rapidly disappearing, such as the secret forging of swords, the cutting of stone implements, the celebration of old established festivals, down to the latest incidents in Corea and Formosa, thus insuring many readers by the wide range of subjects it embraces. Occasionally, advertisements of English goods appear in a cloud of Chinese ideographs, for instance the figure of an English watch printed on colored paper to arrest attention and Japanese articles of manufacture are recommended in the same European method."
