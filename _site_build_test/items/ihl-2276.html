Description:
This print by Sakamoto Hanjirō is one of six he created for the twelve print series Sketches from the Stage, conceived by Yamamoto Kanae (1882-1946) who also contributed six prints. In this print we see the actor Sawamura Tosshi VII (1860-1926) in the role of 松平吉峯, which can be read Matsudaira Yoshimine. All of the prints in the series were of actors appearing at the Imperial Theater (Teikoku Gekijō) which had opened in March 1911.
Yamamoto Kanae, the artist who conceived of this series, is credited with creating the seminal work launching the sōsaku hanga (creative print) movement, a self-carved and self-printed woodcut portrait known as Fisherman (Gyofu 漁夫) in 1904.
In an attempt to revitalize Japanese prints he formed the Tokyo Print Club in 1911 along with Sakamoto. To create this series of actor portraits, consisting of three sets, each set made up of two prints by Yamamoto and two by Sakamoto, Kanae first photographed actors in performance. He and Sakamoto then drew actors from life at the new Imperial Theater which had recently opened on the moat opposite the Imperial Palace. Kanae hoped this series would be a money maker by "appealing to the stylish audience" that would be attending the modern plays at the Imperial Theater. He intended to issue actor portraits from thirty-four new plays, but only twelve prints were produced of the June, July and September performance at the Imperial Theater before the project was abandoned. - Source: Guide to Modern Japanese Woodblock Prints: 1900-1975, Helen Merritt, University of Hawaii Press, 1992, p. 123.
