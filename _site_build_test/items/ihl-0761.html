Description:
This 13th print in the 36 print series depicts farmers harvesting a bountiful autumn crop while Mount Fuji stands guard in all its grandeur. This print is the original issue (first edition between 1939-1940) published by Uchida Woodblock Printing Company as part of the series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji. Later editions eliminated the small telephone pole on the right side of the hill and also changed the title from 豊年の富士 to 實りの秋. To view a later edition of this image please see IHL. 0412, Fall Harvest from the series Eight Views of Fuji.

THIRTY-SIX VIEWS OF MOUNT FUJI - A CELEBRATION OF 2600
Published by Uchida Bijutsu Shoshi between 1939 and 1940, this 36-print set (plus an introductory sheet and a table of contents sheet) by Tokuriki Tomikichirō (1902-2000) is a more modern take on Hokusai's (1760–1849) famous series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji (1826-1833). Its publication was timed to coincide with nationalist celebrations in November 1940 marking Kigen 2600, the 2600th year of Japan's mythical founding as a nation.
Tokuriki's series was extremely popular with domestic and foreign buyers who purchased one thousand copies within a short time after issuance. Various prints from this series were re-printed in subsequent years and subsets of the thirty-six prints were later repackaged into newly named series, such as Eight Views of Mount Fuji and Four Season of Mount Fuji, released in the 1950s. Later printings omit the information in the margin, modify Tokuriki's signature and change the seals below his signature appearing in the image area of the print.
