Description:
This small untitled print of a kabuki actor, with a horizontal tri-fold to allow insertion into a magazine, by the Kyoto nihonga (Japanese style) artist Yoshikawa Kanpō (1894-1979) bears an untypical appearing signature for the artist. The style of the print is reminiscent of those that appeared in the short-lived 1915 magazine Shin Nigao and, confusingly, this collection's print is mounted on a page from the magazine Shin Nigao bearing the title 仁左衛門 (八郎兵衛）松田青風 (Nizaemon (Hachirōbei) Matsuda Seifū), but this print is not by Matsuda Seifū (1902-1983), nor did it appear in any of the five issues of Shin Nigao. It is possible that the actor Yoshikawa is depicting is Nakamura Ganjirō I (1860-1935), the standout actor of his day in the Kyoto theater and a subject who Yoshikawa would portray in other woodblock prints.
Merritt notes in her brief biography of the artist that he "made small hanga portraits of actors ca. 1916 to stimulate revival of hanga in Kansai."[1] This print seems to be consistent with Merritt's comment.
In addition to his painting, Yoshikawa was a print designer, a stage designer and writer. It is believed that Yoshikawa did not produce woodblock prints after 1925.[2]

[1] Guide to Japanese Woodblock Prints: 1900-1975, Helen Merritt and Nanako Yamada, University of Hawai'i Press, 1992, p. 178.
[2] Toledo Museum of Art http://emuseum.toledomuseum.org/objects/49755 [accessed 8-30-23]
