Nagashima Shungyō 永嶌春暁 (active 1870-1905)

Nagashima Shungy (active 1870-1905) Almost nothing is known about the life of Nagashima Shungy. Chaikin and the Tahara Municipal Museum state he was the son of Utagawa Yoshitora (fl. c. 1836-1882).1 Chaikin also writes that he called himself Msai after 1874 and was also known as Utagawa Shungy.2 Most of his extant prints aresenso-eof the Sino-Japanese War orkaika-e(prints of modernization and Westernization).The online database of the Fine Arts Museums of San Francisco, provides the following information on “Shungyo Nagashima”:Nationality: Japanese; Gender: Male; Identified by a Google search. Dates given as active 1882-1905, but print in AFGA collection has been dated to 1906 by Roger Keyes. Son of Utagawa Yoshitora, an Edo artist and a pupil of Utagawa Kuniyoshi, active ca. 1850-1880.3The British Museum lists the artist Nagashima Shungyo as being active 1850-1910.4Shungy and Yoshitora One In The Same?Several sources suggest or imply that Nagashima Shungy, rather than being the son of Utagawa Yoshitora, is one and the same with Yoshitora. This seems to be based upon their reported family and given names Nagashima Tatsugor 34o being the same5 and their both frequently using the art name Msai in signing many of their prints. I think they were two different artists, based upon stylistic and subject matter differences in their prints. In addition, they had different recorded home addresses for the year 1880. Andreas Marks tells us that Yoshitora’s home address in 1877 to 1878 was Kandaku Sudach Yon-banchi, in 1879 Kybashi Matsukawach Roku-banchi, and in 1880 to 1881 Kanda Kajich Roku-banchi. Shungy’s address on this collection’s 1880 print The Glory of the Imperial Flowersis given as Kandaku Sudach Yon-banchi, matching Yoshitora’s 1877-1878 address but not matching his 1879-1881 addresses.6 Signatures Attributed to the Artist Nagashima Shungy Nagashima Msai ga, 1877(British Museum) Nagashima Msai ga Nagashima Msai hitsu Shungy ga Shungy ga1891 Nagashima Msai 33 Nagashima Shungy gaseal reading Yoshiteru 3Shungy sara Yoshtieru ga1890 Msai Shungy, 1894 Msai Shungy, 1894 Msai Shungy, 1894(IHL Cat. #513) Msai Shungy Msai Shungy ga, 1889 Msai Shungy ga, 1888ju Shungy ga1The Sino-Japanese War, Nathan Chaikin, self-published, 1983, p. 61 Tahara Municipal Museum website: Yoshitora (active c. 1850-1880) was born Nagashima and was a disciple of Kuniyoshi.2 Op. site Chaikin, p. 61.3 website of the Fine Arts Museums of San Francisco Although I think the addresses given for artists were somewhat suspect as they sometimes were the address of the publisher.6 Nagashima is also seen using the characters 3 and 3last revision:11/27/2018
